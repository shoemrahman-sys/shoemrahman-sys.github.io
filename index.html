Flagship project

PulsePay: End-to-End Product Analytics & Intelligence Platform

MySQL, SQL, Python, Pandas, Power BI, Statistical Analysis · GitHub repo

Business problem
Where do users drop out of onboarding and activation, why, and would a simplified KYC flow help? Data: 50,000 synthetic fintech users.
Analytical approach
Reusable SQL views for funnel conversion, activation, retention, acquisition-channel performance and product KPIs. Root-cause analysis of KYC failures across devices and app versions. A/B experiment evaluation with a sample-ratio-mismatch check. A four-page Power BI dashboard built with DAX, plus automated Python analytics, evidence preparation and PDF/DOCX reporting.
Findings
  • Android 5.4 KYC completion was 54.69% vs 76.18% on Android 5.3. The dashboard shows 1,420 document-upload errors across 5,129 Android 5.4 attempts (27.69%).
  • The largest funnel drop is between KYC Started and KYC Completed (10,830 users). 19,102 of 50,000 users (38.20%) made a successful transaction.
  • D30 retention rises from 1.10% for users with no early transactions to 16.11% for users with five or more in their first seven days.
  • The 6,032-user KYC experiment was inconclusive: control 62.55% vs treatment 62.48% (SRM p = 0.918; lift p = 0.9575; 95% CI −2.51 to +2.38 pp).
Business implication
Android 5.4's document-upload flow is the first thing to investigate. Against the Android 5.3 baseline, a counterfactual estimate puts the gap at 1,102 fewer KYC completions and 692 downstream activations. These are estimates, not confirmed losses. The simplified KYC flow has not shown a meaningful improvement, and encouraging early repeat transactions is the clearest retention opportunity. Channel scores combine activation, D30 retention and KYC completion; they are not profitability or lifetime value.

Customer Churn Analysis: Olist

Python, MySQL, Pandas, Matplotlib, Seaborn · Jan 2026 – Feb 2026 · GitHub repo

Business problem
Which customers are lost, and where should retention effort go? Data: 96,096 customer-level records from the Olist Brazilian e-commerce dataset.
Analytical approach
Built a customer-level Analytical Base Table (ABT) on customer_unique_id using MySQL and Python. Defined churn as 180 days of inactivity, then analysed behaviour, delivery performance and revenue contribution.
Findings
  • Repeat purchase rate of about 3%, a significant retention challenge.
  • Churn was 85.8% for very-late deliveries vs 59.0% for on-time (26.8 pp gap).
  • The top 10% of customers generated 38.25% of revenue; low-value customers made up 50.27% of churned customers.
Business implication
High-value customer retention and delivery reliability are potential priorities. The delivery and churn link is observational, so it flags a priority but does not prove late delivery caused churn.

Revenue Optimization Analysis: Payment Type vs. Fare

Python, Pandas, SciPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn · May 2026 – Jun 2026 · GitHub repo

Business problem
Do card and cash trips differ in fare? Data: January 2020 NYC Yellow Taxi trips.
Analytical approach
Cleaned and validated 6.4M trips, filtered to 2.78M card and cash trips with 1–5 passengers, then removed non-positive values and IQR outliers, leaving 2.38M. Compared card (n = 1,605,332) and cash (n = 772,886) fares with Welch's two-sample t-test.
Findings
  • Mean fare was $13.70 for card vs $12.25 for cash (t = 165.6, p < 0.001).
  • Card trips were also longer on average: 3.23 vs 2.80 miles.
Business implication
The fare gap may partly reflect trip length. The comparison is observational and unadjusted, not a randomized A/B test, and does not show that payment type changes fares.
